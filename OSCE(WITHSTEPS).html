💡 Pro Tip for OSCE: Always verbalize normal findings! Use: "On [STEP], I observe [FINDING], which is [NORMAL/EXPECTED]." Keep it concise. State the step first, describe what you see/feel/hear, and add the normal interpretation.

🔹 GENERAL PRELIMINARIES (All Stations)

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Introduction & ID:
    • Introduce yourself: "Good morning, I am [Name], a medical student."
    • Confirm patient identity: "May I check your name and date of birth?"
    • Ask preferred form of address.
  2. Explain & Obtain Consent:
    • Explain the procedure in layman's terms.
    • Ask for explicit permission: "Would that be okay?"
    • Assure confidentiality of all information shared.
  3. Hand Hygiene:
    • Perform alcohol-based hand rub or wash hands before patient contact.
    • Repeat after examination completion.
  4. Vital Signs:
    • Record: BP, Temperature, Heart Rate, Respiratory Rate, SpO₂, BMI.
    • Note any abnormalities before proceeding with focused exam.
  5. Chaperone & Privacy:
    • Offer chaperone if examination involves sensitive areas.
    • Ensure curtains closed, door shut, appropriate draping.
    • Expose only the area being examined.
  6. Patient Comfort & Pain Check:
    • Ask: "Is there any area that is painful? Can you point to it?"
    • Examine painful areas LAST.
    • Adjust pillows, ensure room temperature is comfortable.

🔹 ABDOMEN (Order: I → Am → Pe → Pa)

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Preparation & Positioning:
    • Ask patient to empty bladder before exam.
    • Position: Supine with pillow under head AND knees (relaxes abdominal wall).
    • Arms at sides (not behind head—tenses abdominal muscles).
    • Draping: Expose abdomen from xiphoid process to symphysis pubis; drape chest above nipple line and legs below pubis.
    • Stand on patient's RIGHT side for optimal access.
  2. Pain Localization:
    • Ask: "Is there any area of your abdomen that is painful? Can you point to it?"
    • Examine the painful area LAST to avoid guarding throughout exam.
  3. Inspection (from right side, eye level):
    • Skin: Color, temperature, scars (describe location/size), striae (old silver = normal), dilated veins (few small = normal), rashes, ecchymoses.
    • Umbilicus: Contour (inverted normal), location (midline), inflammation, bulges (suggesting ventral hernia).
    • Contour: Flat, flabby, globular, protuberant, scaphoid; symmetry; visible organs/masses (enlarged liver/spleen may descend below ribs).
    • Peristalsis: Observe several minutes if obstruction suspected (normally visible only in very thin people).
    • Pulsations: Normal aortic pulsation at epigastrium.
  4. Auscultation (BEFORE palpation/percussion!):
    • Use diaphragm of stethoscope; place gently on skin.
    • Listen in all 4 quadrants, starting RLQ (ileocecal area most active).
    • Listen 30 sec–2 min per quadrant; note frequency & character.
    • Normal: Normoactive = clicks & gurgles ~5-34/min; borborygmi = stomach growling (normal).
    • Bruits: If HTN, auscultate epigastrium & each upper quadrant; also over aorta, iliac, femoral arteries.
    • Friction rubs: Auscultate over liver & spleen if indicated.
  5. Percussion:
    • Percuss lightly in all 4 quadrants to map tympany (gas) vs dullness (solid/fluid).
    • Liver span (R midclavicular line):
      1. Start below umbilicus in RLQ → percuss upward until dullness (lower border).
      2. Start at nipple line → percuss downward until lung resonance shifts to liver dullness (upper border).
      3. Measure distance: Normal = 6-12 cm.
    • Traube's space: Percuss left lower anterior chest (6th rib → anterior axillary line → costal margin); normal = tympanitic (not obliterated).
    • Splenic percussion sign: Percuss lowest interspace in left anterior axillary line → ask deep breath → percuss again; normal = remains tympanitic.
    • CVA tenderness: Place ball of hand in costovertebral angle → strike with ulnar surface of fist; normal = no tenderness.
  6. Palpation:
    • Light palpation:
      • Dominant hand, fingers together, flat on abdominal wall.
      • Gentle dipping motion (~1 cm depth); glide smoothly across all 4 quadrants.
      • Note: superficial masses, tenderness, voluntary guarding vs involuntary rigidity.
      • Techniques to reduce guarding: help patient relax, palpate after exhalation, ask patient to mouth-breathe with jaw open.
    • Deep palpation:
      • Non-dominant hand pushes dominant hand deeper (~4-5 cm).
      • Use palmar surface of fingers to delineate liver edge, kidneys, masses.
      • Note mass characteristics: location, size, shape, consistency, tenderness, pulsations, mobility with respiration.
    • Liver palpation:
      • Place left hand behind patient parallel to & supporting right 11th-12th ribs.
      • Press left hand upward; place right hand on right abdomen lateral to rectus, fingertips below liver dullness.
      • Ask patient to take deep breath; feel for soft, sharp, regular edge with smooth surface.
      • Hooking technique (obese patients): Place both hands side-by-side below liver border → press in & up toward costal margin → ask deep breath.
    • Spleen palpation:
      • With left hand, reach over & around patient to support & press forward lower left rib cage.
      • With right hand below left costal margin, press in toward spleen.
      • Ask deep breath; feel for tip/edge. Note tenderness, contour, distance from costal margin.
      • Repeat with patient on right side, legs flexed (gravity brings spleen forward).
    • Kidney palpation:
      • Left kidney: Right hand behind patient below 12th rib (fingertips at CVA) → lift to displace kidney anteriorly; left hand gently in LUQ → at peak inspiration, press firmly in LUQ → try to capture kidney between hands.
      • Right kidney: Same technique on right side; may be palpable in thin patients with relaxed muscles.
      • Normal left kidney rarely palpable; right kidney may be; note size, contour, tenderness if palpable.
  7. Special Assessments (if indicated):
    • Ascites: Test for shifting dullness (percuss border supine → roll to side → percuss again; normal = border constant) & fluid wave (press hands midline → tap one flank → feel impulse on opposite flank).
    • Appendicitis: Palpate McBurney point (2 in from ASIS on line to umbilicus) for localized tenderness, guarding, rigidity, rebound.
    • Aorta: Press firmly deep in epigastrium slightly left of midline; in adults >50 y/o, assess width (normal ~2.5 cm, not >3 cm).
    • Bladder: Percuss for dullness above symphysis pubis; palpate dome if distended (smooth, round).

🔹 THORAX & CHEST

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Introduction & Positioning:
    • Explain procedure, obtain consent.
    • Posterior exam: Patient sitting upright.
    • Anterior exam: Patient supine.
  2. Posterior Chest Examination:
    • Inspection:
      • Observe shape & symmetry of chest posteriorly; ask patient to breathe.
      • Note: rigid spine, inflexible sternum/xiphoid (normal bony framework).
      • Check for muscle retractions, use of accessory muscles.
      • Observe respiratory movement: quiet, unlabored, no accessory muscle use.
    • Palpation:
      • Test for tenderness, bulges, depressions, masses, unusual movement.
      • Chest expansion: Have patient fold arms over chest; locate inferior border of scapula; place thumbs at subcostal angle (10th rib); ask deep breath → observe symmetric thumb movement.
      • Tactile fremitus: Patient folds arms; use bony edge of palm/ulnar surface; ask patient to say "tres-tres" or "ninety-nine"; compare symmetric points bilaterally → equal fremitus normal.
    • Percussion:
      • Percuss in ladder pattern, comparing left vs right.
      • Normal = resonant throughout lung fields.
      • Diaphragmatic excursion: Percuss at end-expiration dullness → ask deep breath & hold → percuss new dullness; normal shift = 3-5.5 cm.
    • Auscultation:
      • Use diaphragm; ladder pattern from 1st to 7th ICS posteriorly.
      • Ask patient to breathe deeply through open mouth; one full breath per location.
      • Note duration, pitch, intensity; type of breath sounds; adventitious sounds.
      • Vocal resonance: Ask patient to say "99" repeatedly; listen for changes.
      • Normal: vesicular sounds (I:E = 3:1), soft/low-pitched with rustling quality on inspiration, softer on expiration.
  3. Anterior Chest Examination:
    • Inspection:
      • Note shape/symmetry: clavicles prominent, sternum flat, symmetric expansion.
      • Check for retractions, cyanosis, pursed lips, nasal flaring, clubbing (Schamroth window: diamond present = normal).
      • Observe respiratory movement: quiet, unlabored, no accessory muscle use.
    • Palpation: Same as posterior: tenderness, expansion (thumbs at 10th rib), tactile fremitus ("99").
    • Percussion: Ladder sequence; percuss for liver dullness (R midclavicular) & gastric tympany (L).
    • Auscultation: Same ladder pattern; note vesicular sounds throughout (bronchovesicular only at manubrium/upper interscapular).

🔹 BREAST & AXILLA

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Preparation (Female):
    • Best timing: 5-7 days after onset of menses (breasts less tender/nodular).
    • Explain procedure, obtain consent, offer chaperone.
    • Expose from clavicles to inframammary fold; drape appropriately.
  2. Inspection (Sitting Position):
    • Arms at sides:
      • Skin: texture, color, thickening, prominent pores (normal: no redness/inflammation).
      • Size & symmetry: some difference in breast/areola size is normal.
      • Contour: no masses, dimpling, flattening.
      • Nipples: symmetrical shape/size/direction; no rashes, ulceration, discharge.
    • Arms raised overhead: Assess contour for dimpling/retraction.
    • Hands pressed against hips: Contract pectoralis; reassess contour.
    • Leaning forward (if pendulous breasts): Gravity may reveal subtle retractions not otherwise visible.
  3. Palpation (Supine Position):
    • Place pillow under shoulder on side being examined; arm raised, hand behind head.
    • Use pads of 2nd-4th fingers; apply light → medium → deep pressure in small concentric circles at each point.
    • Lateral portion: Patient rolls opposite hip, keeps shoulders on bed, hand on forehead; palpate axilla → medially → vertical strip to clavicle.
    • Medial portion: Patient lies flat, hand at neck, elbow lifted to shoulder level; palpate straight down from nipple → bra line → back to clavicle → vertical overlapping strips to midsternum.
    • Cover all 4 quadrants + Tail of Spence (axillary extension).
    • Palpate nipple/areola; apply gentle pressure to check for discharge.
    • If nodule found: assess mobility, size, shape, tenderness.
    • Time: Spend at least 3 minutes per breast.
  4. Male Breast Examination:
    • Inspection: nipple/areola for nodules, swelling, ulceration; symmetry.
    • Palpation: areola & breast tissue for nodules.
  5. Axilla Examination:
    • Inspection: Skin of each axilla for rash, infection, unusual pigmentation.
    • Palpation: Patient relaxes arm; cup fingers together, reach high toward apex of axilla.
    • Palpate central nodes first (most palpable); assess tenderness, size (<1 cm normal), mobility.
    • If central nodes abnormal, check: pectoral (anterior axillary fold), lateral (along humerus), subscapular (posterior axillary fold).

🔹 NEUROLOGICAL EXAM

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Mental Status & Cerebral Function:
    • Alertness/Orientation: Assess level of consciousness; orientation to Person, Place, Time.
    • Memory: Remote ("When is your birthday?"), Recent ("What did you eat this morning?"), Immediate (serial 7s, recall objects after 1/5/10 min).
    • Language: Conversation, following commands, reading, writing, naming objects.
    • Cognitive: Arithmetic, R-L orientation, drawing (clock test), gnostic functions (recognize objects by sight/sound/touch).
    • MMSE elements: Registration (3 words), naming, reading ("CLOSE YOUR EYES"), recall.
  2. Cranial Nerves:
    • CN I (Olfactory): Present familiar non-irritating odors; close eyes; compress one nostril; sniff through other; identify smell bilaterally.
    • CN II (Optic): Visual acuity (Snellen/Jaeger, one eye at a time); visual fields (confrontation: test all quadrants with subtle finger movements); fundoscopy (disc margins, AV ratio, hemorrhages).
    • CN III, IV, VI: EOMs (follow fingertip in H-pattern); pupillary reaction (swinging flashlight: EBRTL = Equal, Briskly Reactive To Light; normal 2-4 mm); check for nystagmus, ptosis.
    • CN V (Trigeminal):
      • Corneal reflex: Ask patient to look up/away; approach from opposite side; lightly touch cornea (not conjunctiva) with cotton wisp.
      • Sensory: Test ophthalmic/maxillary/mandibular divisions with light touch, pinprick, temperature; compare bilaterally.
      • Motor: Palpate temporalis/masseter; ask to clench jaw; test pterygoids (open mouth against resistance, move laterally).
    • CN VII (Facial):
      • Motor: Raise eyebrows (upper face), close eyes tightly (observe creases), show teeth (orbicularis oris), smile, puff cheeks; observe nasolabial fold symmetry.
      • Sensory: Taste anterior 2/3 tongue (cotton bud + sugar solution, test one side).
    • CN VIII (Vestibulocochlear):
      • Hearing: Whisper test or finger rub (if abnormal, proceed to tuning fork).
      • Weber: Place vibrating fork on midline forehead; normal = midline (no lateralization).
      • Rinne: Place fork on mastoid → when sound fades, move near ear canal; normal = AC > BC (2:1 ratio).
    • CN IX & X: Ask patient to say "ah" or yawn; observe soft palate rise (symmetric), uvula (midline), posterior pharynx movement (medial curtain-like); test gag reflex.
    • CN XI (Accessory): Trapezius: raise shoulders against resistance; SCM: turn head against resistance (test opposite SCM when head turned).
    • CN XII (Hypoglossal): Protrude tongue (midline = normal; deviates to weak side if lesion); say "lalala" (tongue), "mimimi" (lips), "kakaka" (soft palate); observe for slurring.
  3. Motor System:
    • Inspection: Muscle bulk (atrophy?), fasciculations, involuntary movements.
    • Tone: Passive movement of relaxed limbs: rapid pronation/supination of forearm; flexion/extension of wrist; for lower limbs, place hand behind knee & rapidly raise (normal: ankle drags before lifting).
    • Strength (0-5 scale): Test proximal & distal UE/LE:
      • UE: elbow flexion/extension, wrist extension, grip (squeeze 2 fingers), finger abduction, thumb opposition.
      • LE: hip flexion (press on thigh), hip extension (press posterior thigh), hip abduction/adduction (press lateral/medial knee), knee extension ("lift foot"), knee flexion ("don't let me lift foot"), dorsiflexion, plantar flexion.
    • Pronator drift: Arms extended, parallel to ground, eyes closed, hold 10 sec; normal = no drift.
  4. Cerebellar/Coordination:
    • Rapid alternating movements: Pronation/supination of arms; finger-thumb tapping; tibial tapping (tap ball of foot on examiner's hand/floor).
    • Point-to-point: Finger-to-nose (change directions); heel-to-shin (slide heel down opposite shin while supine).
    • Gait: Casual walk (observe posture/balance); heel walk; toe walk; tandem walk (heel-to-toe).
    • Romberg: Stand feet together, eyes closed 30 sec; normal = maintains balance.
  5. Sensory System:
    • Crude: Light touch (cotton wisp), pain (sharp/dull safety pin), temperature; compare left/right, upper/lower extremities, anterior/posterior trunk.
    • Vibration: 128 Hz tuning fork on bony prominences (DIP joints → more proximal: toes, ankle, patella, iliac crest); compare bilaterally.
    • Proprioception: Hold big toe/finger on sides, move up/down; patient identifies direction with eyes closed.
    • Cortical: Graphesthesia (identify numbers written on palm), stereognosis (identify object placed in hand), bilateral simultaneous stimulation (touch one side → both sides → ask what felt).
  6. Reflexes:
    • Abdominal: briskly stroke each side of abdomen above (T8-T10) & below (T10-T12) umbilicus; note umbilicus movement toward stimulus.
    • DTRs (grade 0-4+):
      • Biceps (C5-C6): arm partially flexed, palm down; thumb on tendon; strike through thumb; observe elbow flexion.
      • Brachioradialis (C6-C7): forearm partly pronated; strike radius 1-2 in above wrist; observe flexion/supination.
      • Triceps (C6-C7): arm flexed at elbow, palm toward body; strike tendon above elbow; observe extension.
      • Patellar (L2-L4): knee flexed; strike patellar tendon; observe knee extension.
      • Achilles (S1-S2): foot dorsiflexed; strike Achilles tendon; observe plantar flexion; note relaxation speed.
    • Plantar (Babinski): Stroke lateral sole from heel to ball, curving medially; use lightest stimulus; normal = big toe plantar flexion (downgoing).
    • Pathologic: Clonus (rapid ankle dorsiflexion → no rhythmic oscillations); Hoffman (flick middle finger → no thumb/index flexion).
  7. Meningeal Signs (if indicated): Neck stiffness; Brudzinski (neck flexion → involuntary hip flexion); Kernig (hip flexed 90° → knee extension causes pain/resistance).

🔹 CARDIAC EXAM

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Introduction & Positioning:
    • Explain procedure in layman's terms; obtain consent.
    • Position: Supine, head elevated 30-45° for JVP assessment.
  2. Inspection:
    • Chest: Observe for deformities (barrel chest, kyphosis), scars (pacemaker, cardiac surgery), symmetry, shape.
    • Precordial impulse: Look for visible pulsations, heaves, thrills.
    • Cyanosis: Check lips, nail beds, extremities.
    • Capillary refill: Press nail bed → release; normal = <2 sec.
    • Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP):
      • Position patient at 30-45°; turn head slightly left.
      • Identify internal jugular pulsation (undulating, non-palpable, varies with respiration/position).
      • Measure vertical height from sternal angle to highest point of pulsation; normal ≤3 cm (or 6-8 cm H₂O).
      • Hepatojugular reflux: Apply firm pressure to RUQ (liver) for 10 sec; observe JVP; normal = transient or no sustained rise >3 cm.
    • Edema/Varicosities: Check lower extremities for pitting edema, varicose veins.
  3. Palpation:
    • Precordium: Use palmar finger pads to palpate:
      • 2nd right & left ICS (aortic/pulmonic areas).
      • Left sternal border (tricuspid area).
      • Apex (mitral area): locate PMI at 5th ICS, 7-9 cm lateral to midsternal line; assess diameter, amplitude, duration.
    • Heaves/Thrills: Palpate for sustained lifts (heaves) or vibratory murmurs (thrills).
    • Peripheral pulses:
      • Radial: compare amplitude, rhythm, symmetry bilaterally.
      • Carotid: palpate ONE at a time (gentle pressure); assess rate, rhythm, amplitude (2+ = brisk/expected), symmetry.
      • Brachial, femoral, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial as indicated.
    • Peripheral signs: Skin temperature, moisture, color; Schamroth window for clubbing (diamond present = normal).
  4. Auscultation ("All Pray To Mama"):
    • Use diaphragm first (high-frequency: S1, S2, systolic murmurs), then bell (low-frequency: S3, S4, diastolic murmurs).
    • Listen in expiration; ask patient to hold breath if needed to reduce breath sounds.
    • Valve areas:
      • Aortic: 2nd ICS, right parasternal.
      • Pulmonic: 2nd ICS, left parasternal.
      • Tricuspid: 4th ICS, left parasternal.
      • Mitral: 5th ICS, midclavicular line (apex).
    • Carotids: Auscultate for bruits (use diaphragm lightly).
    • Normal sounds: S1 (mitral/tricuspid closure) & S2 (aortic/pulmonic closure) crisp, regular rhythm; no extra sounds (S3/S4), murmurs, rubs.

🔹 HEAD & NECK (HEENT Summary)

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Head/Scalp:
    • Inspect/palpate skull: shape (normocephalic), symmetry, masses, tenderness, scars, indentations.
    • Hair: distribution, texture, lesions, signs of loss.
    • Temporal arteries: palpate for pulsations, thickness, tenderness.
  2. Face:
    • Observe symmetry, expression, involuntary movements.
    • Test CN VII: brow raise, eye closure, smile, puff cheeks.
    • Check for drooping (Bell's palsy/stroke).
  3. Eyes:
    • Visual acuity: Snellen (20 ft) or Jaeger (14 in); test one eye at a time.
    • Visual fields: Confrontation test: examiner faces patient, covers one eye each, moves fingers in 4 quadrants; patient reports when seen.
    • Pupils: Size (2-4 mm), shape (round), reaction to light (direct/consensual), accommodation (EBRTL).
    • EOMs: Follow fingertip in H-pattern; check for nystagmus, ptosis.
    • External: Eyelids (edema, ptosis, tremors), lashes (direction, matting), conjunctiva (pink), sclera (white), cornea (transparent), iris (color/flat).
    • Fundoscopy: Red reflex (+ROR), disc margins (distinct), AV ratio (2:3 normal), hemorrhages/exudates (none).
  4. Ears:
    • Inspection: Auricle position/size/symmetry; canal for discharge/swelling; mastoid/tragus tenderness.
    • Otoscopy: Pull pinna up & back (adults); inspect canal (patent, non-hyperemic); TM: cone of light, pars tensa/flaccida, color, landmarks.
    • Hearing: Whisper test; Weber (midline = normal); Rinne (AC > BC = normal).
  5. Nose:
    • External: Symmetry, deformity, alar flaring.
    • Patency: Occlude one nostril, ask to breathe.
    • Internal (otoscope/light): Septum (midline, no perforation), mucosa (pink), turbinates (not congested), polyps, discharge.
    • Sinuses: Palpate frontal/maxillary for tenderness; transillumination if indicated.
  6. Mouth/Pharynx:
    • Lips: color (pink), symmetry, moisture, lesions.
    • Oral mucosa (labial/buccal): smooth, shiny, pink, no pigmentation/lesions.
    • Gums: pink, no swelling/bleeding.
    • Teeth: complete set, no caries, slight discoloration acceptable.
    • Palate: hard (pink, no lesions), soft (pink, rises symmetrically).
    • Tongue: size/shape normal, midline on protrusion, pink with thin white coat, moves side-to-side.
    • Uvula: midline; tonsils: not enlarged, no exudates; posterior pharynx: pink, no exudates.
  7. Neck:
    • Inspection: Symmetry, trachea (midline), thyroid (inspect while swallowing), masses, swelling.
    • Lymph nodes (AAOT SSCCCS): Palpate systematically: pre-auricular, posterior auricular, occipital, tonsillar, submandibular, submental, superficial cervical, posterior cervical, deep cervical chain, supraclavicular. Note size (<1 cm), consistency (soft), mobility, tenderness.
    • Thyroid: Ask patient to extend neck & swallow; palpate isthmus (may be palpable) & lobes (normally not palpable) from behind or front.
    • ROM: Flexion, extension, lateral bending, rotation; full, painless.
    • Carotids: Palpate one at a time; auscultate for bruits.

🔹 SKIN EXAM

📋 Detailed Procedural Steps

  1. Preparation:
    • Explain procedure; obtain consent.
    • Ask patient to change into gown (opening in back); remove clothing except underwear.
    • Offer chaperone; ensure privacy, good lighting.
  2. Systematic Head-to-Toe Inspection & Palpation:
    • General: Color (uniform), temperature (warm), moisture (dry), texture (smooth).
    • Lesions: Document using ABCDE for pigmented lesions:
      • Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter >6mm, Evolving.
      • Describe: number (solitary/multiple), size (mm/cm), color, shape (circular/oval/annular), texture (smooth/scaly/warty), type (macule/patch/papule/plaque/vesicle/bulla), location (distance from landmarks), configuration (grouped/linear).
    • Head/Neck: Scalp, face, ears, neck for rashes, lesions, lymphadenopathy.
    • Upper extremities: Shoulders, arms, hands, nails (clubbing: Schamroth window; cap refill <2s).
    • Trunk: Chest, abdomen, back for symmetry, lesions, rashes.
    • Lower extremities: Thighs, legs, feet, toes for edema (pitting vs non-pitting), discoloration, ulcers, varicosities.
    • Hair: Distribution, texture, scaling, alopecia patterns.
  3. Special Tests:
    • Capillary refill: Press nail bed → release; normal = <2 sec.
    • Skin turgor: Pinch skin over sternum; normal = immediate recoil.
    • Edema grading: 1+ (2mm), 2+ (4mm), 3+ (6mm), 4+ (8mm) depression.
